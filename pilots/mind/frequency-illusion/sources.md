## "The name is recent; the mechanism isn't"

Defensible, with a change of wording.

- **The name:** "frequency illusion" dates from 2005. "Baader-Meinhof phenomenon" is older, from 1994. Both are recent. The 2006 date applies only to the abstract.
- **The mechanism:** both ingredients predate the name. Attention research (Simons & Chabris 1999) and the confirmation-bias literature (Nickerson 1998) were established before 2005.
- **The caveat:** that these two processes together produce this particular experience is Zwicky's explanation in a short abstract. Searches found no controlled experiment isolating the two for the frequency illusion. A popular summary quotes a Macquarie University researcher saying few papers exist, but the page could not be opened, so that remark is unverified.

**Suggested line:** "The name is recent. The attention research behind it isn't." It says what the evidence supports. "The mechanism" alone implies the illusion itself has been dissected in the lab.

- "Coined in 2006."
- "Proven by experiments." Nothing located tests the frequency illusion directly. The evidence beat is a named idea plus attention research.
- "Yellow cars aren't more common." The illusion does not rule out that something really is common. Zwicky: "sometimes your off-the-cuff frequency estimates are right."

## Replication status

Not applicable to the term, which is a name rather than an experimental effect. Selective attention and confirmation bias each have large literatures (see the references below). The specific combination has no located direct test. The reel should present this as a named everyday experience with a plausible explanation.

## Where the brief needs correcting

- **Date:** the brief says Zwicky 2006. The term is from **7 August 2005**. The 2006 item is the abstract that added confirmation bias.
- **Mechanism wording:** "both well established" is true of each process alone, but not of their combination as a tested account of this effect.
- **Evidence beat:** the format calls for "one study", and this pilot has none on the effect itself. Use claim 6 or 7, or a stated "no one has tested this directly".
- **Name card:** "Baader-Meinhof" is the name of a group Britannica describes as carrying out a violent terrorist campaign, and the guardrails bar real people. Spell "frequency illusion" big. If the older name appears at all, give it as a plain text note with no imagery of the group.
- **Premise and ending:** the yellow car is an illustration, and no study used cars. "You'll now notice this everywhere" is a joke that works as a callback, not a finding.
