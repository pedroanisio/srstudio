## The hook: "Most people will defend a choice they never made."

**Not supported as worded.** Three words overreach:

- **"Defend".** Nobody was challenged. Participants were asked why they chose, and they answered. No study read measured what people do under pushback.
- **"Most people".** The 2005 paper's stated numbers are per swap, not per person. It gives no person-level share in its text. The only person-level figures are bars in a supplementary figure (row 5).
- **"Will".** The evidence covers low-stakes choices: faces, jam, tea, survey answers. The 2005 paper itself says "may".

**Plainest true sentence (11 words):** "Swap someone's choice and they'll usually explain it as their own."

"Usually" here means most swaps. In the 2005 study at least 74% of swaps were never detected by any measure (row 3), and reasons were asked for on every swapped trial.

**Shorter hook, usable with one caveat (9 words):** "Most people will explain a choice they never made." This is one word from the brief's line. It is an inference, not a figure any paper states. It rests on three things:

- about two in three shoppers missed their one swap and were asked to justify it (rows 9, 10);
- at least about 80% in every 2005 group missed at least one swap (row 5);
- the 2005 authors' suggestion, offered as a possibility, that "delivering an M report came naturally to most of the participants in our task" (p. 118).

Keep the scope in the caption: simple choices, in experiments.

**What "most" can honestly mean**

| Can mean | Figure |
|---|---|
| Most swaps were not caught at the time | 87% not caught concurrently, 2005 (46 of 354 caught) |
| Most swaps were never caught | At least 74%, 2005; about two in three, 2010 |
| Most people missed at least one swap | About 80–100% by group, 2005, read from bars; 69%, Hall et al. 2012 (moral statements) |
| Most shoppers missed their one swap | About two in three, 2010 |

| Cannot mean | Why |
|---|---|
| Most people missed every swap | About 30–70% by group, 2005, read from bars |
| Most people invented specific reasons | 13.3% of swapped-trial reports were specific confabulation (row 7) |
| Most people argue back when challenged | Not tested |

## Stamp and surprise

**Stamp (12 words):** "Only 13% of swaps spotted at the time. Johansson et al., 2005"

**Alternative (10 words):** "Most swapped photos went unnoticed. Johansson et al., Science 2005"

The brief's example text, "most swaps went unnoticed", is true. Its citation is not: the paper has four authors, so it is "Johansson et al.", not "Johansson & Hall".

**Single most surprising true finding:** of the people who missed every swap, 84% said they would have noticed one (2005 SOM, row 8). The authors call this "choice blindness blindness". The denominator isn't given, the question was hypothetical, and it went to participants who had not yet voiced suspicion.

## Wrong or weaker versions to avoid

| Don't say | Why | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Most people will defend a choice they never made." | See the hook section. | "Swap someone's choice and they'll usually explain it as their own." |
| "Most people never noticed." (2005) | The stated figures are per swap. | "Only 13% of swaps were spotted at the time." |
| "87% of people didn't notice." | 13% is swaps, not people. Same trap as the line above. | "87% of swaps went unnoticed at the time." |
| "74% of people." | Same trap. It is also a floor, not a measurement. | "At least 74% of swaps." |
| "They explain it using details only B has." (as the typical case) | 13.3% of reports. As comedy it's fine. It can't carry a statistic. | Leave the number off the joke. |
| "With total confidence." (as a finding) | Ratings were the same as for real choices, and near the scale's midline. Rieznik et al. 2017 found lower confidence on undetected swaps. | "Just as sure as with a real choice." |
| "People never remember why they chose." | 11.2% of reports referred to the original choice. | "They may be building the reason." |
| "Their brain makes up the reason." | The 2005 paper "warrants no conclusions about the mechanisms behind this effect". | Keep the brief's "may". |
| "This works on anything." | The authors say the effect is bound by choices of great importance: a partner swapped at the altar is something "no one would fail to notice" (2010, p. 59). | Scope it to simple choices. |
| "Swaps work because the options look alike." | Similarity made no difference in 2005 but did in 2010. | Leave similarity out, or name the study. |
| "Swaps change how people vote." | The 2013 Swedish result concerns stated intentions in a survey, and 48% includes 12% who stayed undecided. Rieznik et al. 2017 found no change in voting intentions in Argentina. | "People explained political views they hadn't given." |

## Replication status

- **Direct follow-up with a matching number:** Taya, Gupta, Farber & Mullette-Gillman (2014), *PLoS ONE*, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0108515: 16.9% (27 of 160) concurrent detection, 32 participants, described by the authors as comparable to the 13%. Read as raw text from the journal page.
- **Moral statements:** Hall, Johansson & Strandberg (2012), *PLoS ONE*, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0045457: "a full 69% of the participants failed to detect at least one of two changes". Read as raw text from the journal page; author names from memory, so cite by DOI.
- **Politics, partly conflicting:** Rieznik et al. (2017), *PLoS ONE 12*(2), e0171108, https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0171108: 55% of 4848 manipulated questions undetected online; live, 40% (53) of 131 detected. No change in voting intentions (p = .24). Confidence lower on undetected manipulations (74.20 vs 80.38). The paper's own person-level figures don't add up (48 of 56 given as 88%; 74% and 36% summing over 100), so don't quote them.
- **Same lab, repeatedly.** Much of the record comes from the Lund group, including all three main references.
- Searches found no failed preregistered direct replication. That is a search result, not a registry check. A registered report on social cues (Marchant et al.) turned up in a search, but it could not be opened, so its result is unknown.

## Where the brief needs correcting

1. **The hook and the premise "People defend choices they never made".** See the hook section.
2. **Example stamp "Johansson & Hall, 2005".** It should be "Johansson et al., 2005".
3. **"With total confidence".** Fine as a character note, not as a finding. See the table above.
4. **"Using a detail only B has".** Real, and the paper's own best case, but it is 13.3% of reports. The paper's example is choosing "the blond woman" because she "had dark hair".
5. **Sketch objects.** Photos and jam are tested; shirts aren't. Photos must be drawn and original, per the brief.
6. **"B gets more absurd".** Hall et al. 2010 supports strong contrasts within jam and tea only: Cinnamon-Apple against bitter Grapefruit, and even there up to half were detected.
7. **The turn.** "They may be building the reason, not remembering it" holds with "may". It is an interpretation, not something the papers measured, so it takes no stamp.
8. **Political escalation.** Optional and the riskiest line here. If used, say people explained views they hadn't given (rows 13, 14). Don't say they switched sides.
