# Sources: change blindness

Status key: **full text** = the paper itself was read; **abstract** = only the abstract was read; **secondary source only** = the claim rests on another source, named in the row.

## Stamp and surprise

**Stamp for the gradual change (10 words, abstract-level):** "Large, slow changes can go unnoticed. Simons et al., 2000"

Consequences for the reel:

- It must not say or imply that a study tested a library, a drawn scene, a talking character or a wall.
- It must not say that most viewers miss this reel's change. No source supports that, and one watch-gate viewer can't establish it. Say instead what the stamp says.
- A colour change is the best-supported choice. Under gradual change, blindness was higher for colour than under disruption, while addition or deletion went the other way (row 8).
- A vanishing picture needs care: progressive deletion produces intermediate frames with "easily noticed artefacts, such as transparent objects" (David et al. 2006, p. 255). A fading picture is that artefact, so it may give itself away.
- A different mug has no tested equivalent in the sources read.

If the reel wants to link its demo to the study, this wording holds: "Researchers found the same with photographs."

## Replication status

- David et al. (2006) extend the gradual-change method to faces and report gradual changes as harder to detect than disrupted ones. This is a conceptual extension, and it used only 20 subjects in the gradual condition.
- David et al. (2006, p. 264) note their result differs from Simons et al. on addition and deletion, and attribute that to artefacts.
- Levin, Simons, Angelone & Chabris (2002), *British Journal of Psychology 93*(3), 289–302, https://doi.org/10.1348/000712602760146224, is a follow-up to real-world people swaps by the same group. Citation only; not read.
- Searches found no preregistered direct replication of either main paper. That is a search result, not a registry check.

## Where the brief needs correcting

1. **"Most people miss it" and "Most should miss it".** The first is unsourced as a claim. The second is a hope, and the watch gate can test one viewer only. Keep both off the screen.
2. **"About 8–12 seconds".** Only 12 s is documented. Prefer 12 s.
3. **"A missing picture, a different mug".** See the analogue section. Colour is the best-supported of the three.
4. **"About half didn't notice".** Supported by the abstract ("Only half") and by the count, 7 of 15 (p. 646). The paper says "only half", so that stays the wording.
5. **"People miss bigger things than a wall colour".** Fine as framing, but no study compared the two. Don't attach a citation to that sentence.
6. **Rensink et al.** Confirmed background only: "Identification of changes becomes extremely difficult, even when changes are large and made repeatedly" (abstract). It describes displays with brief blank fields, which is the flicker the reel must not use.
