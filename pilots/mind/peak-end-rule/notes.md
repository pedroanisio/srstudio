## What is claimed, and what is comedy

- The study claim is on the evidence card: 32 male students, one hand 60 s, the other 90 s ending slightly warmer, and
  22 of 32 (69%) chose to repeat the longer one. All from Kahneman et al. 1993, pp. 402-403, rows 1 to 4 of
  `sources.md`.
  The card says "male" because the sample was.
- The dinner is the comedy, not a finding. It follows the short single episode `sources.md` recommends, but no source
  read tests a dinner, so the line "Cold water isn't dinner, but it's worth a try:" says so before the advice.
- The use is worded as a thing to try. No line says it works, and meetings and dates are not mentioned.
- Five stars goes further than the rule. The rule averages the worst moment and the last, so a real diner in this
  position would not be expected to give top marks. This is deliberate exaggeration, and the reel never says the rule
  predicts a score, but "There's a name for that" sits right after it. This is the reel's largest gap between joke and
  evidence.
- "You often reach for" keeps the authors' limit: information is "often not used", not lost (p. 404). The reel never
  says people forget the rest. Rows 11 and 12 also show the overall average predicts about as well, which the reel does
  not mention.
- "The hour barely counts" rests on row 11 (duration "essentially nil"), an abstract only, and is said about the
  reel's own hour, not about a measured dinner.
- The holiday appears only in the caption, as "the evidence is much weaker" (Kemp 2008, Chark 2022). The 682-patient
  trial was read and left out, with all medical material, under the no-health-claims guardrail.
