# Notes: The Spotlight Effect

## Main trigger

Recognition. The reel opens on the behaviour, a hamster looking down at a shirt she regrets, then adds the details
people know from themselves: the clipboard held in front of it, walking sideways, rehearsing the excuse. "It was a
gift." "What was?" is the moment the viewer sees that only she was thinking about it.

## What is claimed, and what is comedy

- The study claim is on the evidence card: students in an embarrassing T-shirt guessed 46% could say who was on it, 23%
  could. Both figures are from Study 1, as listed in `sources.md`.
- The staging is a shirt in a room, which is what was tested. The portrait on the shirt is an invented potato, not a
  person.
- "Since breakfast" is the character's feeling. The study did not measure a day, and no line says it did.
- The turn is "less than you think". It does not say nobody noticed.

## What I would change

- The mechanism runs three lines. A viewer who only wants the joke gets a slower middle than the opening; cutting
  "You are the center of your own attention." would need the beam to carry that idea alone.
- The fox appears only in the row. A pilot with room for a third reaction could use it at the end.
- The shirt sits in front of the arms' upper edge. It reads as a shirt, but a clothing layer under the arms would be
  cleaner if shirts come back in the series.
- The name lands at 37% of the way in, a little ahead of the 40% target. A longer hold on her reaction would close that
  gap, at the cost of pace.
