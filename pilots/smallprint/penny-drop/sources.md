## On screen

| On screen | Source | Year | Checked |
|---|---|---|---|
| A penny tumbles; it tops out around 25 mph ("TOPS OUT AT 25 MPH") | Louis Bloomfield (physicist, University of Virginia), quoted by Natalie Wolchover, "Could a penny dropped off a skyscraper kill someone?", Live Science, <https://www.livescience.com/18832-penny-dropped-skyscraper.html> (republished by Scientific American, <https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/could-a-penny-dropped-off/>) | 2012 (published 2012-03-05) | Page text: pennies "flutter to the ground at a measly 25 mph (40 kph), Bloomfield said", reaching that speed "after only about 50 feet (15 meters) of descent" |
| "MythBusters tried it in 2003. Busted." ## Not on screen, used for the script

- The building's height comes from the Empire State Building's official facts page,
  <https://www.esbnyc.com/about/facts-figures>: 1,250 ft (380 m) to the highest occupied floor. It is not stated in
  the reel.

## Demand: people ask this

- **Autocomplete.** Google's suggest endpoint (`suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=can a penny`)
  returned "can a penny dropped from the empire state building kill you" on 2026-11-02. The card on screen uses those
  words.
- **A public video with over 1M views.** Veritasium, "How Dangerous is a Penny Dropped From a Skyscraper?",
  YouTube `16Ci_2bN_zc`. Its watch page reported a `viewCount` of 19,765,540 on 2026-11-02. The reel follows none of
  its beats: it has no experiment, no wind tunnel and no person.

## Safety

No one is ever drawn below the drop. The only thing the penny lands on is the worktop. The script says "Please don't
check.", and the post says "Please don't test it."
